## Data problems (need reruns)
1. **Shelter leak:** `sex` (spayed/neutered) is largely recorded after the outcome, since California requires it
   before adoption. On its own it gives AUROC 0.75. `intake_condition` also looks post-outcome. Drop both and rerun
   shelter.
2. **Contamination of the frontier LLMs was never checked.** Opus's cutoff is June 2026 and the data runs Jan–Aug
   2026. Opus's GitHub zero-shot AUROC of 0.87 beats a per-repo-rate oracle. Rerun Gemini and Opus zero-shot on
   post-cutoff samples (`contamination_check.py` already builds them).
3. **Opus's API route varies by task.** GitHub and inspections went 100% through OpenRouter, HN was mixed, and the
   rest went through Anthropic, so thinking settings may differ. The cache key ignores the route. Rerun on one route.
4. **GitHub:** `draft` is the state at closing (0/48 drafts merged). Titles may have been renamed at merge. The sample
   is the top 200 by relevance, not random. facebook/react is silently missing because a `gh` error is swallowed.
   Some bot PRs slipped through.
5. **Random split within one period:** examples can come after the test cases. This matters for flights (`date` is a
   feature) and inspections (the same businesses appear in both pool and test; 13 texts are duplicated). Use a
   temporal split.
6. **Crimes:** police-initiated offence types exist because of an arrest (narcotics are 98% arrested).
7. **Smaller issues:**
   - Gemini parser: 2 synthetic batches answered on a 0–1 scale were divided by 100. No conclusion flips.
   - 402 credit errors were logged as "does not fit in context".
   - The "both" arm fits Platt on predictions from 1-case requests and applies it to 25-case requests.

## Missing baselines (framing)
- **Jev in-context vs plain logistic regression on the same labels:** significantly better on 0/8 at k=50–200. Its
  best pipeline beats LR on 1/8. The study's own option 4 was never answered.
- **A per-group label-rate lookup matches Jev in context.** On GitHub, the per-repo rate ties it.
- **Against a constant (the label average), at k=100:** Jev in-context beats it on 2/8, examples + Platt on 5/8,
  Gemini on 5/8, Opus on 7/8.
- **The base-rate sentence:** told Jev beats predicting the stated rate on only 2/8. Jev moves only partway toward
  the stated rate. A label-free logit shift of its own scores to the stated rate is significantly better on 5/8
  tasks. Jev's scores are too compressed, not overconfident (Platt slopes of 2–3 where it ranks well).
- **The shuffled-label control inflates "learning":** in-context minus zero-shot AUROC is significant on only 5/8
  tasks, and not on crimes, flights or HN.
- **"Gemini beats Jev zero-shot on 6/8":** these are the same 6 tasks where a constant beats Jev.
- **Synthetic tasks:** Platt brings Jev and Gemini to about 0.030 MSE. "7–8× closer" is an MSE ratio (about 2.8× in
  RMSE), and LR is the correctly specified model there.
- **Stacking:** Jev's score doesn't significantly help TabPFN on 7/8 tasks; the wins were TabPFN's.
- **Untested claims:** "Platt matters most with noisy labels"; "split when over the context limit" (never exceeded);
  base-rate sentence + examples. The base-rate sentence also used an oracle-ish pool rate (about 1,200 labels).

## Framing and fairness
- TypeSafe's claim ("higher confidence means higher accuracy") is about ordering, and mostly holds zero-shot. ECE is
  listed as a metric but never reported.
- **Jev's real wins are underplayed:**
  - Stack Overflow free text beats tuned TF-IDF + LR (0.74 vs 0.66, significant);
  - it got the tellin effect right;
  - its synthetic ranking is on par with Gemini's.

## What survives
- Jev learns from labels on 5/8 tasks versus zero-shot.
- Its zero-shot means are far from the base rates.
- Its free-text advantage over trained text models.
- Gemini and Opus beat a constant more often than Jev does.
- Arithmetic prior shift beats telling the model the rate.
- On structured data, in-context examples ≈ logistic regression on the same labels.
